What MasterScope Meter does

Straight from the app

It listens to Spotify (or any app you choose) directly, before your laptop's sound effects can change it, so it reads the true master. Or listen to the whole computer: YouTube, a video, a file in any player. Nothing to set up.

The whole song

Press Measure at the start and Stop at the end: integrated loudness, true peak, PLR (density), loudness range and the loudest moment, with a one-line verdict on how hard it was limited.

What streaming does to it

How much Spotify, YouTube, Apple Music, Tidal, Amazon Music and Deezer turn the song down or up, so you can see why a louder master doesn't play louder.

Compare in MasterScope

Save a song and press Compare: MasterScope opens with it as your reference, and when you drop in your mix you see the differences side by side.

Its shape and tone

A graph of the song's loudness from start to finish, with the −14 and −16 LUFS lines the services play at, and its average tone, flat for pink noise like MasterScope's.

Numbers, never audio

The Meter keeps the measurements and nothing else: no recording, no copies of the music. Song names come from the Spotify window or the YouTube tab, to label what you save.

Get true readings

  1. Turn off normalisation in Spotify (Settings, Normalise volume), or you measure Spotify's turn-down instead of the master.
  2. Set the player's own volume to 100%. Your computer's main volume doesn't matter.
  3. Listen to the app, not the whole computer. The Meter picks Spotify automatically when it plays; choose another app under Listen to. Laptops process the sound on its way to the speakers (Waves MaxxAudio, Dolby, Realtek speaker tuning), and listening to the whole computer measures that processing too.

Install on Windows

  1. Run the MasterScope tools installer and tick MasterScope Meter (and Liminal if you like).
  2. Open MasterScope Meter from the Start menu and play a song.
  3. Press Measure at the start of the song and Stop at the end.

Works with Liminal

In Liminal, Tools → Measure a song measures a reference track playing in your DAW, with a button to use its loudness or density as Learn's target. The Meter does the same for anything on your computer. See Liminal.
