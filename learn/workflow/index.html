A mastering workflow

This is the loop that works with MasterScope and Liminal. It starts in the mix, because that is where the biggest gains are.

  1. Analyse the mix. Bounce it with no limiter on the master and open it in MasterScope. Fix anything in "What to work on" first: mud, harshness, wide bass, energy dips, and whatever the Louder at −14 panel says the limiter would be fighting.
  2. Put Liminal last on the master. Start from the factory preset Streaming: punchy (PLR 10), or Lookahead mode with attack around 10 ms and release around 60 ms. Add Bass first only if the low end is triggering the limiter.
  3. Set the ceiling. −1 dB with the ceiling mode on Streaming (or All codecs if the track goes to SoundCloud, DJ pools or Apple Music). Liminal keeps the decoded peaks under it, so you no longer need to guess how much lower to go for loud masters.
  4. Choose your density. Click the target and pick a PLR. For a full, competitive sound, aim for 9 to 11.
  5. Press Learn and play the song once. Liminal listens, then renders what it heard at different thresholds and sets the one that lands on your target.
  6. Save the report. Press Report and save the .json, then drop it on MasterScope to see the result, the gain reduction over time and your settings. No second play-through needed. (Record does the same for a live play-through after you change settings by hand.)
  7. Check the punch, then Optimise. If the Punch reading shows the limiter taking more than 3 or 4 dB off the hits, press Optimise and try the top setup. Mix Doctor shows which part of the mix makes the peaks, so you know what to fix at the source.
  8. Listen with Match, Delta and Opus. Match hears your mix at the same loudness, Delta only what the limiter removes, Opus what a streaming listener gets. The Spectrogram's Delta view shows which frequencies are being squashed.
  9. Export and check the final file. Open the bounced master in MasterScope: true peak, codec peak, density and tone. Then load a reference on the Compare tab.
