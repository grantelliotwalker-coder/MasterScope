Why does it sound quieter than the rest?

Streaming services turn every song to about the same loudness, so what you hear is density: how far the peaks stand above the body of the track (PLR). Too open sounds small next to commercial releases; too dense sounds squashed.

Why is it free?

Because we wanted to put a genuinely powerful tool in everyone's hands, so anyone can learn and master the art of mastering. No paywalls, no account, no ads in the plug-in, and your audio never leaves your computer.

Questions

Is it really free?

Yes. MasterScope and the Liminal plug-in are free to use with no account and no time limits. The website carries a few music-related sponsors; the plug-in has none.

Is my audio uploaded anywhere?

No. MasterScope analyses your file inside your browser. Nothing is sent to a server, and the plug-in collects nothing.

What files can I drop?

WAV, AIFF, FLAC or MP3 (lossless files give the most accurate results), and Liminal's report files (.json).

Does Liminal work in my DAW?

Liminal is a VST3 plug-in for Windows 10 and 11 (64-bit), so it works in Ableton Live, FL Studio, Cubase, Reaper, Studio One, Bitwig and other VST3 hosts. A Mac version is on the way. Downloads are coming soon, on the Liminal page.

Should I master to −14 LUFS?

Not necessarily. Pick the density you like (a PLR around 9 to 11 for punchy, competitive music) and let the loudness follow. Services turn louder masters down, so the extra loudness only helps where nothing normalises, like DJ sets, Bandcamp and downloads. Read the chapter on density →

How is this different from other limiters?

Liminal learns the setting for your target from one play-through, checks the peaks through real streaming encoders, and sends a report to MasterScope, which tells you what to change next. Most tools only meter.

Streaming platforms

Targets are each service's published default. When a quiet track would be turned up, services generally only raise it as far as its peaks allow, so this assumes a −1 dBTP limit. Listeners can change or switch off normalisation, and services update their policies from time to time.
