Loudness & Dynamics

Integrated Loudness
True Peak

Integrated loudness is measured per ITU-R BS.1770-4: K-weighting filters, 400ms gated blocks with 75% overlap, absolute gate at −70 LUFS and relative gate at −10 LU below the absolute-gated mean. Commercial masters typically target around −14 to −9 integrated LUFS with true peak below −1 dBTP. True peak here uses 4x linear-interpolation oversampling as an approximation of the standard's polyphase filter — close, but a dedicated meter may read slightly differently on inter-sample peaks.

Vectorscope & Phase Correlation

Phase Correlation
—
−1 (out of phase)0+1 (mono)

The scope shows L/R as a Lissajous pattern: a vertical line means perfectly mono/correlated, a wide circle means very wide or phase-incoherent stereo. Correlation near −1 is a real problem, it means content will cancel out in mono playback (phones, clubs, some Bluetooth speakers).

Stereo Field Map — Width by Frequency

The same idea as the spectrogram, but for stereo width instead of time: every frequency in the track summed up and plotted by how centered (mono) or spread (wide) it is. Bass generally belongs low on this chart — wide bass is a common mono-compatibility problem.

Streaming Platform Loudness Preview

Most streaming platforms normalize playback loudness to their own target. If your integrated loudness is louder than the platform's target, they'll turn your master down to match — so mastering hotter than a platform's target buys you nothing there, it just eats into your peak headroom for no benefit. If you're quieter than the target, most platforms turn you up, which can also raise your true peak above 0 dBTP if you had little headroom to begin with.
