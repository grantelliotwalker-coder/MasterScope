Liminal 1.0 · user guide

How to use Liminal

Everything in Liminal, from installing it to the last button. If you only read one part, read the quick start: it gets a song release ready in about the time it takes to play it. Hover over any control in the plug-in for a short explanation too.

1. Install

Downloads are coming soon. These steps are for when the download is live on the Liminal page.

Windows

  1. Run the installer. It copies Liminal.vst3 to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3, the standard folder every VST3 host looks in. If you have a zip instead, copy Liminal.vst3 into that folder yourself.
  2. Open your DAW and let it rescan plug-ins (most do this on start-up; in some you need to press Rescan in the plug-in settings).
  3. Find Liminal under the maker MasterScope, in the Dynamics or Effects category.

Liminal needs Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit, and a DAW that loads VST3 plug-ins: Ableton Live, FL Studio, Cubase, Reaper, Studio One, Bitwig and most others.

macOS

A Mac version (VST3 and AU, for Logic too) is on the way.

Uninstall

Delete Liminal.vst3 from the VST3 folder. Your own presets are kept in Documents\MasterScope\Liminal\Presets, so reinstalling brings them back. Delete that folder too if you want them gone.

2. Quick start

  1. Put Liminal last on your master bus, after any EQ, compression or saturation. Nothing that changes the level should come after it.
  2. Pick a starting point. The default is fine. For most music, the preset Streaming: punchy (PLR 10) is a great start.
  3. Choose a target in the Learn panel (bottom right). Click the value: pick a loudness such as −14 LUFS, or a density such as PLR 10. Not sure? Use PLR 10. See Learn and targets.
  4. Press Learn and play the song, the whole thing or at least its loudest section. Then press Stop. Liminal works out the threshold that lands on your target at your ceiling, checks it through the real Opus and Vorbis encoders, and sets it.
  5. Listen. Click Match in the Listen panel to hear your unlimited mix at the same loudness, then Out again. If the limited version sounds smaller or the drums lose their snap, try Optimise or a higher PLR.
  6. Bounce your master as usual. Optional: press Report, save the .json file and drop it on MasterScope to check it against every platform and get suggestions.

3. The window

  • Top bar: presets (arrows step through them), Save, Delete, Undo, Redo, A/B, Record session (the red dot), dark or light theme, full screen and About (ⓘ), which has links to this guide and What's new.
  • Display (top left): eight views, chosen with the tabs. See Views.
  • Controls (bottom left): the limiting mode and the knobs for that mode, plus M/S, Sidechain, Bass first and Adaptive release.
  • Faders (middle): Threshold, the GR meter and Ceiling, then the ceiling mode, Auto makeup, Output and Stereo link.
  • Right: the Loudness panel, Listen buttons and the Learn panel.
  • Bottom bar: latency, the stereo correlation meter, Dither and Reset meters.

Drag the bottom-right corner to make the window any size (the display gets the extra room), or press full screen. New instances open at the size you last used.

4. Learn and targets

Learn is how Liminal sets itself (the first time you open Liminal, the display shows these steps). Press Learn, play the song, press Stop. While you play, it listens; when you stop, it renders what it heard through hidden copies of itself at different thresholds, picks the one that hits your target at your ceiling, and checks the result through real streaming encoders. Then it restarts the loudness readings so they reflect the new setting. Undo puts the old threshold back.

Two kinds of target

TargetWhat Learn aims forUse it when
Loudness (LUFS)An integrated loudness: −14 for Spotify, YouTube, Tidal and Amazon; −16 for Apple Music; around −9 for club.A distributor, label or client asks for a number.
Density (PLR)A peak-to-loudness ratio: how dense the master is. 9 to 11 is punchy and competitive; 12 and up is open; under 8 sounds squashed.Streaming. The services turn every song to the same loudness, so density is what listeners actually hear.

While Learn calculates, a bar under the button shows its progress. Afterwards the Loudness panel shows what Learn measured (Learned: −14.0 LUFS) until enough of the song has played again for its own readings to settle; the line under the big number says how long it has been measuring.

Use the − and + buttons for small steps, or click the value for the full list. Learn's own report (the Report button) is the same session file Record makes, rendered from what Learn heard, so one listen gives you the setting and the report. More on why density matters in Learn: density.

5. Ceiling and codecs

The Ceiling is the highest true peak the output can reach. −1.0 dBTP is the usual target for streaming; use −2.0 for very loud masters. Under the faders, the ceiling mode decides what the ceiling protects against:

  • True peak: the classic ceiling. The file's own peaks, including those between samples, stay under it.
  • Streaming (the default): also keeps the peaks a listener gets after Opus (YouTube) and Ogg Vorbis (Spotify) encoding under the ceiling. Encoders remove the very top of the spectrum and that pushes peaks up; Learn measures how much for your song with the real encoders and allows for it. No extra latency.
  • All codecs: adds a 1 dB margin for MP3 and AAC (SoundCloud, DJ downloads, Apple Music), whose encoders overshoot a little more.

That's why, after Learn in Streaming mode, the true peak can sit well under the ceiling while the Codec peak sits right at it. More in Learn: peaks.

6. Modes

ModeHow it worksBest for
LookaheadSees peaks coming and turns down smoothly before they arrive.Most mastering. The cleanest mode, and where Bass first works best.
Punch GuardA lookahead limiter into a clipper: the tips of the hits go to the clipper, so the mix isn't turned down around every drum hit.Dense, drum-led music where you want loudness and snap. Use 2x or 4x oversampling.
MultibandLows and highs are limited separately, split at the Crossover, then a final stage holds the sum.Mixes where a big kick or bass makes everything else duck.
BrickwallInstant, zero-latency limiting.Tracking and live monitoring, or deliberately hard limiting with oversampling.
Soft ClipRounds peaks off for a warmer, driven sound. Drive sets how hard.Character and saturation, or before another limiter.

8. Listen

  • Out: Liminal's normal output.
  • Match: your unprocessed mix at the same loudness as the output. Flip between Out and Match to judge what limiting does to the sound without louder fooling your ears.
  • Delta: only what the limiter takes away. Ideally faint transient ticks; clicks, crunch or bass in here are what listeners lose.
  • Opus: the output through a real Opus encoder and decoder at 128 kbps (what YouTube streams), in real time. About 30 ms late, for listening only.
  • Ref: your reference track at your output's loudness, following the playhead. Load one on the Optimise tab.

Listen settings are for monitoring only: they aren't saved in presets and never change your bounce. Set it back to Out before you export.

Reset meters (bottom right) clears the loudness, peak and gain-reduction readings so you can measure from the top of the song again.

10. Views

  • Limiter: the last 10 seconds going in, what's left after limiting and the gain reduction, with the threshold dashed.
  • Loudness: short-term loudness over time against your target.
  • History: gain reduction over a longer stretch. A line that never returns to zero means constant limiting.
  • Spectrum: the output's frequency balance, scaled so pink noise is flat, with Average, Peak hold, Input and Reference overlays. Hover for frequency, note and level; double-click to clear.
  • Spectrogram: frequency over time in 2D or 3D, with a Delta view of only what limiting removes. Freeze holds the picture.
  • Mix Doctor: after Learn, shows which part of the mix makes the peaks the limiter fights, how much a little peak control there would save, and what to change in the mix.
  • Optimise: see the next section.

11. Optimise, reference and release pack

All three live on the Optimise tab and use the audio Learn heard, so press Learn and play the song first.

Optimise

Tries about 30 setups (different attack and release, Multiband, Punch Guard, Bass first, Brickwall) on the loudest 30 seconds. Every one is learned to your target and ceiling mode, so they're all equally loud and dense, and they're ranked by punch. The top ones are checked with the real encoders. Press Apply to use one: Liminal sets the controls and re-learns it on the whole song. Undo goes back.

Reference track

Load reference takes a WAV, AIFF, FLAC or Ogg of a released track you like. Liminal measures its loudness, peaks, density, punch and tone. Ref in Listen plays it at your loudness, the spectrum can overlay its tonal balance, and Use its density sets Learn to its PLR so your master is as dense as the reference.

Release pack

Make pack learns and writes four masters from one listen: Streaming −14 (safe on every codec), Club −9, Apple Music −16 and YouTube −14 (Opus-safe). Each is a 24-bit WAV with its MasterScope report. Choose a name and the files go next to it.

12. Presets, A/B and reports

  • Presets: 14 factory presets, from Streaming: clean (PLR 11) to Broadcast −23 (EBU R128), plus your own. Save stores the current settings; Delete removes a preset of yours.
  • Import preset (at the bottom of the preset menu) loads a preset file, such as the one MasterScope suggests from your report. It's copied into your presets and can be undone.
  • A/B: two sets of settings to compare. Click the other letter to switch; changes stay in the slot you were on.
  • Undo / Redo cover every change, including Learn and Apply.
  • Record session (●): press, play the song, press again. Save as .json for MasterScope or .csv for a spreadsheet. Learn's Report button gives you the same file without a second play-through.
The round trip with MasterScope
  1. Learn, then press Report and save the .json file.
  2. Drop it on MasterScope: you get the platform-by-platform verdict, a timeline, and suggested changes to your Liminal settings.
  3. Download the suggested preset, use Import preset in Liminal, and Learn again.

13. Troubleshooting

Liminal doesn't show up in my DAW.
Check that Liminal.vst3 is in C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3, that your DAW is 64-bit and has VST3 enabled, then rescan plug-ins.
Optimise, Mix Doctor or the release pack say to Learn first.
Optimise, Mix Doctor, the release pack and Report all use the audio Learn heard. Press Learn, play at least a few seconds (ideally the whole song), and press Stop.
The loudness doesn't match my target.
The readings only cover what has played since the last reset. Press Reset meters and play the whole song, or press Learn again if you changed something before the limiter.
It sounds squashed.
Try a higher PLR target (11 or 12), Punch Guard with 2x oversampling, or Optimise. Then check Mix Doctor: often one part of the mix makes most of the peaks.
The kick makes everything duck.
Turn on Bass first (Lookahead mode), or try Multiband.
High CPU or latency.
Lower Oversampling or turn off Linear phase while working, and turn on 4x on export so the bounce is still clean. Brickwall has no limiting latency at all, for tracking.
